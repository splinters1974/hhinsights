How it works

What the analyser does with your data

Each step, from reading your file to the report. In the analyser, every finding links to its full calculation.

Last updated 14 November 2026

The analyser checks every reading, works out how much the site uses when open and when closed, and prices the difference at your rates. It tells you where to look; someone who knows the building decides what to change.

1. Reading your files

One Excel or CSV file per meter, in any of the common layouts. The analyser shows how it read each file, including whether readings were kWh or kW. Files longer than a year use the last 12 months by default; trends still use the whole file.

2. Checking the data

Before showing figures, the analyser looks for missing readings (left out, never counted as zero), zero days, clock changes, duplicate rows and extreme spikes. Each site is rated Good, Check or Poor, and you can exclude bad readings with a note of why.

3. Opening hours

Every site starts with standard office hours, marked unconfirmed until you check them. Set hours for all sites or one at a time, including closures, bank holidays and night shifts. Findings based on unconfirmed hours get lower confidence.

4. Base load and use while closed

Base load is the level demand stays above 95% of the time (the 5th percentile): servers, fridges and anything on standby.

Closed-hours excess is use while closed, above the base load. Sites are ranked by it because it's usually the cheapest to cut, through timers and controls.

5. Costs and annual figures

Costs use your unit rates (flat or by time of day) and exclude standing and capacity charges. They show what a pattern costs now, not a promised saving. Annual figures are scaled from valid readings: marked indicative under about 11 months of data, and not shown under six.

6. Findings

Each finding has two ratings. Impact is how much it matters. Confidence is how reliable the evidence is. Check a high-impact, low-confidence finding before quoting it.

7. Capacity and headroom

Enter your agreed supply capacity (kVA, from your bill) and a power factor. The analyser works out how much extra constant load the site could take. Only your network operator can confirm it.

What it can't tell you

  • Main meter data shows when electricity is used, not which equipment uses it.
  • Figures aren't adjusted for weather or occupancy.
  • Carbon uses one conversion factor you enter.
