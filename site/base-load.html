Base load: what never switches off

Every building has a level of demand it never drops below. Because it runs every hour of the year, small reductions add up.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Base load is the electricity demand a building never drops below, overnight and at weekends as well as during the day. Each kilowatt of it runs for 8,760 hours a year: 8,760 kWh, about £2,190 at 25p/kWh. Measure it from half-hourly data, find out what makes it up, and switch off what doesn't need to run.

How to measure it

Take every valid half-hourly reading, convert to kW (kWh divided by half an hour), and find the level demand is above 95% of the time: the 5th percentile. That's the analyser's base load reference.

Why not the lowest reading? One meter dropout or a power cut would set it, and the true floor would look lower than it is. The analyser shows the lowest half hour alongside, so you can see the difference.

What makes it up

  • IT: servers, network switches, phone systems
  • Refrigeration: kitchen fridges, freezers, vending machines, water coolers
  • Pumps and fans that run continuously
  • Hot water heaters and boilers kept on standby
  • Security, fire systems and emergency lighting
  • Equipment on standby, and plant left running by mistake

How to reduce it

  1. Walk the building at night or at a weekend and note everything that's on.
  2. Sort it: must run, could run less, shouldn't be on.
  3. Fix the easy ones: timers on water heaters and vending machines, switch-off routines, consolidating servers.
  4. Watch the base load month by month. A step up usually means something new was installed or left on; the analyser flags lasting changes like this.

Is it worth it?

At 25p/kWh, every 1 kW cut from the base load is worth about £2,190 a year. A 0.5 kW cut from a heater left on, at the same rate, is about £1,100. The arithmetic is simple because base load runs every hour of the year.

Questions people ask

What is a typical base load?

It depends on the building: a data-heavy office or a site with refrigeration will have a much higher floor than a school. Compare a site against itself over time, and against similar buildings in your estate.

Why is my base load higher at weekends than overnight on weekdays?

It shouldn't be. If it is, something is switching on at weekends (heating frost protection, a scheduled process or a booking). Check the heatmap for the weekend pattern.

How much does 1 kW of base load cost?

It runs 8,760 hours a year, so 8,760 kWh. At 25p/kWh that is about £2,190 a year; multiply by your own rate.
