Out-of-hours electricity use

Electricity used when a building should be shut is often the cheapest consumption to cut. Here is how to find it in half-hourly data, and what usually causes it.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Out-of-hours use is electricity used while a building should be shut. Compare each half hour against the opening hours and subtract what never switches off (the base load); what's left is the out-of-hours excess. It's usually the cheapest use to cut, because the fix is a setting, not new equipment.

What counts as out-of-hours

Any half hour outside the building's opening hours: evenings, nights, weekends, bank holidays and closure periods. Not all of it is waste. Some load has to run all the time, such as servers, fridges, security and emergency lighting. That's the base load.

The useful figure is the excess: use while shut, above the base load. In the analyser it's called the closed-hours excess, and it's the figure sites are ranked by.

How to find it

  1. Set the opening hours properly. Everything depends on them. Include bank holidays, closure weeks and regular evening or weekend use.
  2. Look at the heatmap. Every half hour of the year in one picture: open hours show as a bright band, and anything bright outside it is out-of-hours use.
  3. Split it by day type. Weeknights, Saturdays, Sundays and bank holidays usually have different causes.
  4. Check the edges of the day. Demand that rises an hour or two before opening, or stays up after closing, points to time clocks and optimum-start settings.

What usually causes it

  • Heating, ventilation and cooling running to the wrong schedule, or not knowing about bank holidays
  • Lighting left on, or on timers that no longer match the building's use
  • A small evening or weekend booking running the whole building
  • Plant left in hand (manual override) after maintenance
  • IT, catering and hot water equipment left on rather than on timers

How to cut it

Most of the fixes are settings: building management system schedules, time clocks, holiday calendars and switch-off routines. Start with the biggest block, often weekends or a recurring evening, and check the half-hourly data a month later to see whether it worked. The analyser's before-and-after comparison is built for exactly this.

Questions people ask

Is all out-of-hours use waste?

No. Some load must run all the time. The out-of-hours excess, use while shut above the base load, is the part worth investigating.

Why do opening hours matter so much?

They decide which half hours count as closed. If a building is genuinely used on Saturdays and the calendar says it is shut, Saturday use shows as out-of-hours. Confirm the hours before acting on the figures.

How quickly can I see whether a change worked?

With half-hourly data, within weeks. Compare the same days before and after the change; the analyser has a before-and-after view for this.
