Electricity use in healthcare buildings

Hospitals and many care settings run 24/7, so out-of-hours use isn't the right question. Day against night, and peaks against capacity, are.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Continuous operation with a daytime rise. Clinics, surgeries and administrative buildings on the same estate keep office-style hours and should be treated like offices.

What to check first

  • Night demand that is close to daytime demand in non-clinical areas
  • Weekend use in outpatient and administrative buildings
  • Peaks close to supply capacity before electric heating or EV charging is added
  • Steps in base load after new equipment or a refurbishment
  • Sub-meters for different buildings or wings, where available

Setting the opening hours

Set 24/7 buildings as always open, so the analysis focuses on base load, peaks and changes over time, and give clinics and offices their real hours.

These are starting points, not rules. Every site is compared with its own calendar, so the hours matter more than the sector.

Questions people ask

Can out-of-hours analysis work for a hospital?

For the main 24/7 building, compare day against night and track base load instead. For clinics and offices on the estate, out-of-hours analysis works as for any office.

Why does supply capacity matter so much in healthcare?

Replacing gas heating with heat pumps adds a large winter load. Check how close current winter peaks already come to the agreed capacity first.
