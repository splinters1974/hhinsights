Electricity use in industrial sites and warehouses

Shift patterns make the calendar the most important setting. Get it right and shutdowns, compressed air and refrigeration show up clearly.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Demand follows shifts: a clear step up when a shift starts and down when it ends. Planned shutdowns should drop close to the base load.

What to check first

  • Compressed air left on overnight and at weekends; leaks show as a steady load when production stops
  • Refrigeration and chillers running harder than needed
  • Shutdown weeks that never get close to the base load
  • Lighting in high-bay warehouses outside shift times
  • Peaks against capacity before adding EV charging for vans or forklifts

Setting the opening hours

Enter shift times, including overnight shifts, and planned shutdowns, so the analysis separates production from everything else.

These are starting points, not rules. Every site is compared with its own calendar, so the hours matter more than the sector.

Questions people ask

How do I spot compressed air leaks?

Look at demand when production has stopped. If the compressors keep cycling with nothing using air, the steady load is often leaks.

Can the analyser handle night shifts?

Yes. Opening hours can run past midnight, and each day of the week can have its own hours.
