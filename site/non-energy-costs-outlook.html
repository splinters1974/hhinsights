Where non-energy costs are heading

Published forecasts and decisions for each non-energy charge on business electricity bills: what is confirmed, what is draft, and what is still undecided.

By Paul Johnston, Managing Director, Kinetic Energy Consulting · Last updated 14 November 2026

Published figures point to higher balancing charges from April 2027: NESO's draft tariff is £21.67/MWh for April to September 2027, up from £13.74/MWh, driven by constraint costs and wholesale prices. The Climate Change Levy rises to 0.827p/kWh and is then frozen, while the Renewables Obligation falls to 0.369 certificates per MWh. These are published forecasts and decisions from the bodies that set the charges, not our predictions.2,7,5

Balancing costs are forecast to rise

NESO's draft tariffs for 2027/28 are £21.67/MWh for April to September 2027 and £18.42/MWh for October 2027 to March 2028. NESO attributes the rise to higher constraint costs (up 32% for the first period) and higher wholesale prices (up 33%).2 Final tariffs are published each December.1

Relief for manufacturers moves costs to others

From 2027 the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme will cut electricity costs for eligible manufacturers by up to £40/MWh, by exempting them from the indirect costs of the Renewables Obligation, Capacity Market and Feed-in Tariffs.6 Exempt electricity doesn't carry these costs, so they are spread over everyone else. The government's own calculation shows the 2027/28 Renewables Obligation level for other users rising from 0.369 to 0.394 certificates per MWh if the scheme is in place in time.5

Networks: a new price control from 2028

Ofgem has set out how it will judge the distribution network operators' plans for 2028 to 2033, and will approve the final settlement by the end of 2027. It expects demand on distribution networks to rise, while promising tighter cost controls to stop unnecessary investment being recovered through bills.11 Separately, NESO's CMP440 proposal would reintroduce demand transmission charges from 1 April 2027.10 Check for Ofgem's decision before relying on it.

Levies that track wholesale prices

The CfD levy moves the opposite way to wholesale prices: it fell to £0/MWh from 5 November 2026 and is set at £2.98/MWh for January to March 2027.9 If wholesale prices stay high, it may stay low; if they fall, it will rise.

The temporary VAT zero rate ends in June 2027

The 0% rate for qualifying supplies in Great Britain, including charities' non-business use and very small business supplies, runs from 1 October 2026 to 30 June 2027.12 Most business electricity is unaffected and stays at 20%.

What you can do

  • Check your contract to see which non-energy charges are fixed and which are passed through when they change.
  • Look at your use between 4pm and 7pm on winter working days. The Capacity Market charge is based on it.13 Your half-hourly data shows exactly how much falls in that window.
  • Check your agreed capacity. Transmission charges for low-voltage sites are banded by it. See supply capacity.
  • Cut use while closed. Most charges are per unit, so every unit saved reduces them. Start with out-of-hours use.

Questions people ask

Will non-energy electricity costs go up in 2027?

Some will. NESO's draft balancing charge for April to September 2027 is £21.67/MWh, up from £13.74/MWh, and the Climate Change Levy rises to 0.827p/kWh. The Renewables Obligation level falls. Final balancing tariffs are published in December.

What is the British Industrial Competitiveness Scheme?

A scheme starting in 2027 that cuts electricity costs by up to £40/MWh for eligible manufacturers by exempting them from the indirect costs of the Renewables Obligation, Capacity Market and Feed-in Tariffs.

Will the Climate Change Levy keep rising?

The electricity rate rises to 0.827p/kWh from April 2027 and is then frozen at that level from April 2028, according to HMRC.
