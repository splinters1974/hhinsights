Proving a saving worked

You changed a setting or replaced some equipment. Half-hourly data can show whether it made a difference, if you compare like with like.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Compare matched periods before and after the change: the same days of the week, the same kind of day (open, closed, holiday), and enough weeks to smooth out noise. Look at the part of demand the change should affect, such as base load or out-of-hours use, rather than the total bill.

Compare like with like

  • Same kind of day. Compare weekdays with weekdays and holidays with holidays. A before period full of bank holidays will flatter the after period, or the reverse.
  • Long enough. Four to eight weeks either side is usually enough to see a real change in half-hourly data. A single week can be swayed by one unusual day.
  • Close in time. The nearer the two periods, the less the weather and the seasons get in the way.
  • Leave out the change itself. Skip the days when the work was being done.

Measure the right thing

Pick the measure the change should move:

ChangeLook at
Heating or ventilation schedulesOut-of-hours use, and demand around opening and closing
Switching off equipment overnightBase load
LED lightingOpen-hours demand
Holiday settings in the building controlsUse during closures

Total consumption mixes the change with everything else that happened, such as occupancy, weather and new equipment, so a real saving can disappear in it.

Weather and seasons

Electric heating and cooling follow the weather. If the before period was colder than the after period, some of the drop is weather, not the change. For heating-related changes, compare the same weeks a year apart as a cross-check, or normalise with degree days if you have them.

In the analyser

  • Did a change make a difference? on the Base load tab compares matched weeks either side of a date you choose.
  • Compare periods on the Heatmap tab compares any two periods side by side.
  • Persistent changes on the Unusual days tab flags lasting shifts on its own, so you can check whether a change shows up when you'd expect.

For formal measurement and verification of savings, the International Performance Measurement and Verification Protocol (IPMVP) sets out recognised methods.

Questions people ask

How long should I wait before checking?

Four to eight weeks of data after the change, compared with the same length of time before it, is usually enough to see a real change.

Why has our bill not gone down?

A saving in one place can be hidden by changes elsewhere: occupancy, weather, prices or new equipment. Compare the specific measure the change should affect.

Can this replace a formal M&V report?

It is a good check, but formal measurement and verification (for example under IPMVP) sets a baseline, adjustments and reporting up front.
