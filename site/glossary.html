Glossary

Simple definitions of the terms used in half-hourly electricity data and on this site.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Half-hourly (HH) data is a record of the electricity a meter measures in every half hour: 48 readings a day, usually in kWh. The terms you will meet most often are kWh (energy, what you are billed for), kW (how fast electricity is being used at a given moment), base load (the demand that never switches off) and kVA (the unit your supply capacity is set in).

Agreed supply capacity
The maximum demand, in kVA, an electricity connection is set up for. It appears on larger electricity bills and is also called authorised or available capacity. More.
Annualised figure
A yearly figure scaled up from the valid readings available. It is used when the data covers less than a full year or has gaps.
Base load
The level of demand a building rarely drops below: what never switches off. The analyser measures it as the 5th percentile of half-hourly demand. More.
Closed-hours excess
Electricity used while a building should be closed, above its base load. The analyser ranks sites by it. More.
Coverage
The share of expected half-hourly readings that are present and valid.
Degree days
A measure of how cold (heating degree days) or warm (cooling degree days) a period was. Used to compare energy use across periods with different weather.
DNO
Distribution network operator. The company that runs the local electricity network and your site's connection to it. The first two digits of your MPAN show which one. More.
Half-hourly (HH) data
A record of the electricity used in every half hour: 48 readings a day.
Heatmap
A chart of every half hour in a period, with one column per day and one row per half hour, coloured by demand. Also called a carpet plot. More.
kVA
Kilovolt-ampere. A measure of apparent power, and the unit supply capacity is set in. kVA = kW ÷ power factor.
kW
Kilowatt. A measure of power: the rate electricity is being used at a given moment. Divide a half-hourly reading in kWh by 0.5 to get the average kW for that half hour.
kWh
Kilowatt-hour. A measure of energy, and what you are billed for. Using 1 kW for one hour is 1 kWh.
Load factor
Average demand divided by peak demand. A low load factor means demand has short, high peaks.
Market-wide half-hourly settlement (MHHS)
The move to settle every electricity meter in Great Britain using half-hourly data. More.
MPAN
Meter point administration number. The 13-digit number on the bottom line of the supply number on an electricity bill. It identifies the supply point. More.
Peak demand
The highest half-hourly average demand in a period, usually in kW or kVA.
Power factor
The ratio of real power (kW) to apparent power (kVA), between 0 and 1. Equipment such as motors lowers it.
Profile class
The first two digits of the top line of the supply number. 00 means the supply is settled half-hourly; 01 to 08 mean use is estimated from standard profiles. More.
Settlement
The process that works out how much electricity each supplier's customers used, and when, so suppliers pay the right amount for it.
Time-of-use tariff
A tariff with different unit rates at different times of day, such as day and night rates.
