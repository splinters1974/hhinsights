Supply capacity and kVA

Before adding heat pumps, EV chargers or new plant, check how close your highest half hours already come to your agreed supply capacity.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Your agreed supply capacity is the maximum demand, in kVA, your connection is set up for. Convert your half-hourly peaks from kW to kVA (divide by the power factor), compare them with the agreed capacity, and the gap is your headroom for new load such as heat pumps and EV chargers.

What agreed supply capacity is

Larger supplies have an agreed capacity, sometimes called authorised or available capacity, set in the connection agreement and usually shown on the bill in kVA. You typically pay a charge for it every day whether you use it or not. Going over it can bring extra charges, and the network operator may ask you to increase it.

kW and kVA

Meters measure energy (kWh); half-hourly data gives average power in each half hour (kW). Capacity is set in kVA, which also counts reactive power. The link is the power factor:

kVA = kW ÷ power factor. At a power factor of 0.95, a 95 kW peak is 100 kVA. If your bill shows reactive power charges, your power factor is probably lower than 0.95.

Working out headroom

  1. Find your highest half hours over at least a year, so winter peaks are included.
  2. Convert them to kVA with your power factor.
  3. Keep a safety margin, for example 10%, below the agreed capacity.
  4. The headroom is the largest constant load you could add without going over the margin in any half hour.

Half-hourly averages smooth out very short spikes, so this is a screen, not confirmation from your network operator.

Heat pumps and EV charging

A heat pump's electrical input is much lower than its heat output: at a coefficient of performance of 3, a 150 kW heat output draws about 50 kW. It runs hardest in winter, when building peaks are usually highest. EV charging overnight lands where the site is quietest. The analyser lets you test a new load at a chosen time of day and season against your actual half-hourly peaks.

Questions people ask

Where do I find my agreed supply capacity?

On your electricity bill or connection agreement, in kVA. Your supplier can confirm it.

What power factor should I use?

If you do not know it, 0.95 is a reasonable starting assumption. A lower value is likely if your bill shows reactive power charges.

Is half-hourly data enough to plan a new connection?

It is a good first screen, but half-hour averages hide very short peaks. For a significant new load, talk to your network operator before committing.
