Worked example: one primary school

A year of half-hourly data from a primary school, set up with its term dates and run through the analyser. The school is made up; every figure is what the analyser reports.

Last updated 14 November 2026

Once its term dates were entered, this school used 31% of its electricity while shut: about £1,260 a year above what never switches off, at 25p/kWh. The biggest single problem was October half term, when demand was more than double what it should have been for an empty building.

Made-up data. Download the file and load it into the analyser to follow along. Set the building type to School and add the term dates below as closures.

Setting it up

School holidays are never assumed: the analyser asks you to enter them. For this school:

  • Building type: School (open 07:00 to 17:30 on weekdays)
  • Six closures: Easter, May half term, summer, October half term, Christmas and February half term
  • Hours ticked as confirmed

Without the term dates, holiday weeks would count as normal school days, and a heating fault in a holiday would hide as normal use.

The year at a glance

Two things stand out: a pale block across the whole of October half term, and thin stripes after 18:00 on two evenings a week in term. How to read a heatmap.

What the analyser found

1. Heating plant on through October half term

The three largest out-of-hours days of the year were all in half term, about 100 kWh each above base load. The analyser flagged a lasting change: demand from 25 October was at least 15% above its expected level on five of nine days, on average more than double. The pattern (07:00 to 17:00 on weekdays, nobody in) points to plant running to its term-time schedule.

2. Evening lettings

Weekday evenings account for the largest share of the out-of-hours excess: 2,765 kWh above base load. The stripes on Tuesday and Thursday evenings are a regular hall letting. Lettings are fine, but they should be in the calendar as open hours, and it's worth checking whether a hall booking is lighting and heating more of the building than it needs.

3. Holidays and weekends

The holidays themselves added 1,157 kWh above base load, most of it in October. Saturdays and Sundays were close to base load (556 kWh and 526 kWh above it across the year), which is what you'd hope for.

What we'd do next

  1. Add the holiday calendar to the building management system, so heating and ventilation switch off for half terms as well as the long holidays.
  2. Add the lettings to the analyser's calendar, then look at what the hall booking switches on.
  3. Check the next half term with the analyser's before-and-after comparison to see whether the fix worked.

Questions people ask

Do I have to enter term dates?

Yes. The analyser never assumes them, because they vary by school and by year. Paste them in as closures; without them, holiday weeks count as normal school days.

Should a school use nothing in the holidays?

No. The base load continues (IT, fridges, security). Holiday use above the base load is what to look at.

Is this real data?

No. The school and its readings are made up, so the method can be shown without anyone's real data.
